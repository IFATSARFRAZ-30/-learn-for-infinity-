Why starting early beats saving more

Compound growth, with a slider you can break. · 4 min read · interactive

Two people save the same amount every month. One starts 10 years later. The gap is bigger than you'd guess.

With compounding, your earnings earn their own earnings. The longer that snowball rolls, the faster it grows. Set your numbers (savings run until age 60):

–you put in
–growth did the rest
–cost of waiting 10 years

Rule of 72: divide 72 by the yearly return to estimate how many years money takes to double. At 10%, about 7 years.

This is an illustration with a steady assumed return. Real returns vary and can be negative, and this is not investment advice.

Remember this

  • Time in the market beats amount saved, early on.
  • Waiting costs more the higher the return.
  • A small amount started now beats a big amount started later.
