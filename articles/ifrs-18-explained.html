What IFRS 18 changes, explained simply

The income statement gets sorted into clear boxes. Try sorting items yourself. · 5 min read · interactive

Every company's income statement used to be a different shape. IFRS 18 fixes that.

IFRS 18 was issued in April 2024 and applies to annual periods starting on or after 1 January 2027. It replaces IAS 1. Its goal is simple: make profit statements easier to compare. The big changes:

  • Three clear categories in profit or loss: operating, investing and financing (plus income taxes).
  • New required subtotals: operating profit, and profit before financing and income taxes.
  • Management-defined measures (like "adjusted EBITDA") must be explained and reconciled in the notes.

The new layout

Revenue · cost of sales · operating expenses
= Operating profit
Investing: interest and dividends earned, gains on investments
= Profit before financing and income taxes
Financing: interest on borrowings
Income taxes
= Profit for the year

For a typical company (banks and insurers follow special rules), which category does each item land in?

Operating is the leftover box: anything that isn't investing, financing or tax goes there. That makes the first subtotal consistent across companies.
