Why companies fail despite having good products

A good product is not enough. Tap the cards, then test your own business. · 5 min read · interactive

Kodak built the first digital camera in 1975. Then Kodak went bankrupt. How?

Great products die all the time. The product was rarely the problem. Tap each card to flip it.

📷 Kodak
Its engineers invented the digital camera in 1975. Tap to see what went wrong.

It guarded its profitable film business instead of pushing the thing that threatened it. Filed for bankruptcy in 2012.

📼 Blockbuster
Thousands of stores, millions of customers. Tap to see what went wrong.

Customers disliked late fees and streaming removed the need to visit a shop at all. Filed for bankruptcy in 2010.

📱 Nokia
The world's biggest phone maker for years. Tap to see what went wrong.

Strong hardware, but rivals sold a whole ecosystem of apps and software. Sold its phone business to Microsoft in 2014.

The pattern: the product was fine, the business around it wasn't: the model, the timing, the cash, or the customers' habits changed. Now check your own venture. Tick what is true:

Remember this

  • Ask "what could make my product unnecessary?" before a rival does.
  • Cash buys time to fix mistakes. Protect it.
  • Depending on one customer, one supplier or one habit is a hidden risk.
