How subscription businesses actually make money

Spoiler: the first month usually loses money. · 6 min read · interactive

You pay Rs 1,000 a month for an app. Does the company make money from you?

Not at first. Meet FitBox, a made-up gym app. To get you to sign up it spent Rs 5,000 on ads. You pay Rs 1,000 a month and about 80% of that is left after running costs. So it needs six months of you just to get its ad money back. Everything depends on one question: how long do customers stay?

Four numbers run the whole game

Lifetime (months) = 1 ÷ churn
LTV = price × margin ÷ churn
Payback (months) = CAC ÷ (price × margin)

Churn is the share of customers who cancel each month. LTV is what a customer brings in over their whole stay. CAC is what it cost to win them. A common rule of thumb is that LTV should be at least 3× CAC. Now break FitBox yourself:

–average stay
–LTV ÷ CAC
–payback

Watch 100 new customers month by month. Dots fade as people cancel.

Test yourself

FitBox: price Rs 1,000, margin 80%, churn 10%, CAC Rs 5,000. That is LTV ÷ CAC of 1.6, which is weak. The founder can do one thing. Which helps LTV ÷ CAC more?

A wins. Halving churn doubles LTV to Rs 16,000, a 3.2× ratio. Cheaper ads only reach 2.3×. Keeping customers is usually worth more than finding new ones.

Remember this

  • Subscriptions are slow-burn: you lose money first and earn it back over time.
  • Small changes in churn move LTV a lot, because it sits under the fraction.
  • Healthy rule of thumb: LTV ≥ 3× CAC and payback under about 12 months.
