State Bank held its policy rate at 11.5%. Three members wanted a rise

9 Oct 2026 · 2 min read

The State Bank of Pakistan kept its policy rate at 11.5% at its 14 September meeting. Minutes published on 9 October show the vote was seven to three: seven members for holding, and three for raising the rate by 100 basis points to 12.5%.

  • Policy rate: 11.5% (unchanged)
  • Vote: 7 to hold, 3 to raise by 100 basis points
  • Headline inflation: 9.2% in July, 11.1% in August
  • Core inflation: 8.7%, up from 8.4%

The committee pointed to the blocked Strait of Hormuz hurting trade, higher energy prices, and LNG prices rising after supplies from Qatar fell. It also cited the risk of a strong "Super El Nino" weather event damaging crops, and higher inflation expectations among consumers, businesses and forecasters. The bank still expects inflation to ease toward 7% by June 2027, but says the risks to that view have grown.

What it means

The policy rate is the rate the central bank uses to steer borrowing costs across the economy. Holding it means loans do not get cheaper or dearer because of the central bank. A split vote suggests some members saw inflation as the bigger threat than slowing growth.
