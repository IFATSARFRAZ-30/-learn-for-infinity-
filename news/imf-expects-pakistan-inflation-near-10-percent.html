IMF expects Pakistan inflation near 10% in FY27

13 Oct 2026 · 2 min read

The IMF expects Pakistan's inflation to average 9.9% in the 2026-27 fiscal year, above the government's 8.2% target. It keeps its growth forecast at 3.5%, below the government's 4% target.

  • Inflation forecast FY27: 9.9% (government target 8.2%)
  • GDP growth forecast FY27: 3.5% (government target 4%)
  • Current account deficit: about 1% of GDP, roughly $4.4 billion

The IMF estimates growth was 3.6% in FY26. It says strong overseas remittances helped keep the current account deficit under control. It also warned that conflict in the Middle East could lift energy prices and add to inflation, which could put pressure on State Bank interest rates, and it urged Pakistan to phase out the prime minister's petrol subsidy gradually rather than expand it. Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb expects growth to exceed 4%.

What it means

Forecasts from the IMF and the government often differ, and the gap matters: the government plans its budget on its own numbers, so if inflation runs higher than planned, interest rates tend to stay high for longer and borrowing stays expensive for businesses.
